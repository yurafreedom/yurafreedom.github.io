A transparent case study of a private-company take-home assignment, the financial logic behind it and how I used AI to learn the analysis.

I completed an analytical take-home challenge related to Allegro marketplace sales, advertising and SKU performance as part of an application to a private business. It was a learning experience; I do not claim that I was employed by Allegro or that recommendations were implemented in production.

[ 02 / ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK ]
TASK 01

Where is sales plan slipping?

Decompose monthly shortfalls into product categories and SKU-level drivers: traffic, CTR, conversion, pricing, stock availability and ad delivery.

TASK 02

Is turnover profitable?

Separate revenue from contribution profit, include product costs and fees, calculate advertising break-even points and prioritize actions by expected margin.

TASK 03

What action changes the outcome?

Use data to propose testable next steps, define success metrics and revisit forecasts rather than choosing actions from intuition alone.

[ 03 / FORMULAS & REASONING ]

Numbers should drive decisions.

Revenue = Views × CTR × Conversion × Price
Contribution = Revenue − COGS − Fees − Delivery − Ads
Break-even ROAS = 1 / Pre-ad Contribution Margin

These are analytical frameworks, not evidence of realized client revenue. The original company dataset is not published here without confirmation of permission.

Prioritize the causes

In the exercise, different SKU shortfalls suggested different hypotheses: price competitiveness, advertising reach, and unavailable stock. Each requires different verification before changing prices, bids or purchasing plans.

Estimate the recoverable future

The challenge required distinguishing lost historical sales from sales that might still be influenced during the remaining month, and explicitly stating assumptions behind each scenario.

[ 04 / FIRST 30 DAYS PLAN ]

How I would set up the rhythm.

DAYS 01–05

Audit data access, reconcile top SKUs, define baseline, build a daily plan-versus-actual view.

DAYS 06–12

Introduce daily exception detection and a weekly unit-economics report; investigate the highest-value losses.

DAYS 13–20

Track actions as experiments; measure actual profit and cohort-level SKU launch performance.

DAYS 21–30

Stabilize reporting cadence, review sourcing opportunities and prepare next-month priorities.
